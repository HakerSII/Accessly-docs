% Slide 7 – go-to-market: first customers, channels, regulation as demand, scaling
% (criteria: deployment potential and scalability 20 %, business model 20 %).
\begin{frame}{Go-to-market: from hotels and clinics to a second city}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
      \begin{block}{First customers and channels}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{Hotels and medical tourism clinics} (502 accommodation and 1,117 health venues in the catalogue):
                guests from abroad ask about accessibility before booking; the “After treatment” profile.
          \item \textbf{Cultural institutions and event organisers} (420 culture venues, halls and arenas):
                a statutory accessibility duty and participants' questions before every event.
          \item \textbf{Channels:} tourism organisations and hotel associations, the medical tourism cluster,
                the city as a reference; a community partner to verify data.
        \end{itemize}
      \end{block}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \begin{alertblock}{Tailwind: regulation}
        \footnotesize
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{Polish Accessibility Act} (2019): public entities must ensure and report accessibility.
          \item \textbf{Digital Accessibility Act} (2019) and WCAG~2.2 for public websites and apps.
          \item \textbf{European Accessibility Act} (applies from June 2025): e-commerce, transport and online services
                must inform about accessibility.
        \end{itemize}
        \vspace{0.3mm}
        Institutions need data, venues an argument, cities a tool without their own IT.
      \end{alertblock}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{1mm}
  \begin{exampleblock}{Scaling to other cities}
    \footnotesize
    A new city = a configuration file (name, bounds) + an OpenStreetMap extract (available for all of Europe) + the city's open layers, if any.
    Catalogue, profiles, statuses and panels are shared; a local partner moderates.
  \end{exampleblock}

  \note[item]{Sales order: first the segment that already loses money on missing information (hotels, clinics), then institutions with a statutory duty, finally the city as a white-label customer.}
  \note[item]{Exact names if the jury asks: Act of 19 July 2019 on ensuring accessibility for persons with special needs; Act of 4 April 2019 on the digital accessibility of public websites and mobile applications; Directive (EU) 2019/882 (European Accessibility Act).}
  \note[item]{Community partner: a disability organisation that verifies the first data and tests accessibility with users. The 4-language interface supports the medical tourism segment.}
  \note[item]{Honestly about scaling: city layer configuration lives in code today (\texttt{layers.py}), not in the city file; that is the first step for a second city.}
\end{frame}
